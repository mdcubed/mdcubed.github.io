Our science

From molecular mechanisms to therapeutic discovery.

We combine structure-based drug design, molecular simulations, and free-energy methods to understand molecular mechanisms and guide therapeutic discovery.

Explore our research themes ↓

Research theme 01

Mechanism to prediction

We aim to turn dynamic molecular mechanisms into predictive descriptors of receptor function. Our approach combines membrane-embedded molecular dynamics simulations, free-energy calculations, and machine learning grounded in biophysical features. These features include time-resolved contacts, activation switches, helix motions, hydration, and allosteric communication.

Our research spans serotonin, dopamine, and histamine receptors, alongside incretin receptors and an orphan receptor. The goal is to connect simulation-derived features with experimental pharmacology and evaluate predictions on previously unseen ligands and variants.

Building on published results

Published work provides a practical foundation for this approach. In a collaborative study, structure–activity analysis and molecular modeling helped identify β-arrestin-biased serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonists. These experimentally characterized ligands demonstrate how molecular insight can guide the discovery of compounds with distinct signaling profiles, supporting the feasibility of our mechanism-informed design strategy.

Poulie et al., Journal of Medicinal Chemistry (2022) ↗

Research theme 02

Photoswitchable ligand design

Photoswitchable ligands change their molecular configuration in response to light, offering a way to control biological activity in space and time. We are developing a computational platform to support their design and prioritization.

Our approach combines molecular modeling with data-driven predictions to compare photoswitchable candidates and guide experimental studies. The aim is to make light-controlled ligand design more predictive and accessible across biological targets.

Building on published results

Our published PSW-Designer platform establishes a working foundation for photoswitchable ligand design. This open-source computational platform supports the design and virtual screening of photopharmacological ligands, demonstrating that the core design workflow can be implemented and shared. Building on this result, we aim to extend predictive capabilities and broaden applications to new targets.

Simon et al., Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling (2023) ↗

Research theme 03

Allosteric modulation

We investigate how positive and negative allosteric modulators (PAMs and NAMs) can enhance or reduce receptor responses. Our interests span G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) and neurotrophin receptors, with a focus on understanding selectivity and the modulation of neuronal signaling.

By combining molecular modeling and simulations with experimental collaborations, we aim to understand modulator action and guide the discovery of new compounds. This work explores opportunities to regulate neurotrophin signaling and neuronal plasticity, with potential relevance to brain disorders.

Open science and training

Our research program emphasizes reproducible simulation and machine-learning workflows, open tools, and close collaboration with experimental researchers. We train students to formulate mechanistic questions, build reusable research infrastructure, and communicate across disciplines.

Our training and education activities connect with the Center for Pharmaceutical Data Science Education (CPDSE).
