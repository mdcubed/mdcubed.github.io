The researchers connecting molecular mechanisms to drug discovery at MD³.

Principal Investigator

Icaro Ariel Simon

Icaro is a computational pharmacologist with a PhD in Pharmaceutical Sciences from the University of Copenhagen and an MSc in Chemistry from UFRGS, Brazil. His experience spans an industrial PhD at SARomics Biostructures and postdoctoral research at Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam and the University of Copenhagen. He combines receptor pharmacology, molecular simulations, free-energy calculations, and open-source tool development.

GitHub

Icaro’s research

From molecular mechanisms to predictive drug design

My research asks how ligands, genetic variants, and receptor isoforms reshape molecular dynamics and signaling. I combine structure-based design, molecular dynamics, free-energy methods, and interpretable machine learning to study selectivity and signaling bias. My research program connects precision GPCR pharmacology with light-controlled ligands and allosteric modulation of GPCRs and neurotrophin receptors, aiming to translate mechanistic insight into practical design principles.

PhD Student

Pablo Baceiredo Macho

Pablo has a background in biochemistry from the Autonomous University of Madrid and training in bioinformatics and computational biology at the University of Copenhagen, supported by a “la Caixa” Foundation fellowship. His experience combines experimental neuroscience with computational structural biology, Python-based analysis, molecular dynamics, and machine learning.

Pablo’s research

Predicting ligand- and variant-specific GPCR signaling

My research connects molecular dynamics simulations with machine learning to investigate how genetic variation and ligand binding affect GPCR signaling. Focusing on dopamine receptors, the project aims to extract dynamic interaction patterns, activation features, and allosteric communication pathways that help predict functional differences across ligands, variants, and receptor isoforms.

MSc Student

Simon Guldbrandt Kristensen

Simon is an MSc student in pharmacy interested in the intersection of pharmaceutical science and data science. Alongside his university studies, he has pursued additional Python training and developed an interest in molecular modeling and computational drug discovery. He is motivated by open science and the development of reusable research workflows.

Simon’s research

Structure-guided discovery of modulators of an orphan receptor

My project aims to identify peptide and small-molecule modulators of an orphan receptor through structure-based modeling and pharmaceutical data science. It explores peptides derived from the receptor’s extracellular loop and uses ensemble virtual screening to identify candidate small molecules. Molecular dynamics simulations will help prioritize candidates that may stabilize inactive-like receptor conformations for experimental follow-up.

Irem’s research

Molecular dynamics exploration of biased signaling in two histamine receptor isoforms

My project explored how receptor isoforms and ligand interactions influence biased signaling in histamine receptors. Using molecular dynamics simulations, I investigated receptor flexibility and interactions with signaling proteins to better understand the structural basis of pathway selectivity.

Guest Researcher

Julien Reißmann

Julien studied Biochemistry at Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf, where he completed both his BSc and MSc. He carried out his master’s thesis at the Department of Drug Design and Pharmacology in Copenhagen as an Erasmus exchange student, defended it in October 2026, and continues in the group as a guest researcher. His experience in Düsseldorf’s Computational Biochemistry group includes structure-based drug design and molecular dynamics simulations.

Julien’s research

Ligand-dependent signaling in class B incretin GPCRs

My research explores how peptide ligands influence signaling in class B incretin GPCRs. I combine molecular dynamics simulations with data analysis to investigate the molecular basis of potency and bias between G protein and β-arrestin signaling, with the aim of informing peptide ligand design.
