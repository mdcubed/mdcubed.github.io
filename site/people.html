The people behind the science.

Meet the researchers connecting molecular mechanisms to drug discovery at MD³.

Principal Investigator (PI)

Icaro Ariel Simon

Icaro is a computational pharmaceutical scientist with a PhD in Pharmaceutical Sciences from the University of Copenhagen and an MSc in Chemistry from UFRGS, Brazil. His experience spans an industrial PhD at SARomics Biostructures and postdoctoral research at Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam and the University of Copenhagen. He combines receptor pharmacology, molecular simulations, free-energy calculations, and open-source tool development.

My research

From molecular mechanisms to predictive drug design

My research asks how ligands, genetic variants, and receptor isoforms reshape molecular dynamics and signaling. I combine structure-based design, molecular dynamics, free-energy methods, and interpretable machine learning to study selectivity and signaling bias. My research program connects precision GPCR pharmacology with light-controlled ligands and allosteric modulation of GPCRs and neurotrophin receptors, aiming to translate mechanistic insight into practical design principles.

My research

Predicting ligand- and variant-specific GPCR signaling

My research connects molecular dynamics simulations with machine learning to investigate how genetic variation and ligand binding affect GPCR signaling. Focusing on dopamine receptors, the project aims to extract dynamic interaction patterns, activation features, and allosteric communication pathways that help predict functional differences across ligands, variants, and receptor isoforms.

My research

Structure-guided discovery of modulators of an orphan receptor

My project aims to identify peptide and small-molecule modulators of an orphan receptor through structure-based modeling and pharmaceutical data science. It explores peptides derived from the receptor’s extracellular loop and uses ensemble virtual screening to identify candidate small molecules. Molecular dynamics simulations will help prioritize candidates that may stabilize inactive-like receptor conformations for experimental follow-up.

My research

Molecular dynamics exploration of biased signaling in two histamine receptor isoforms

My research

Ligand-dependent signaling in class B incretin GPCRs

My research explores how peptide ligands influence signaling in class B incretin GPCRs. I combine molecular dynamics simulations with data analysis to investigate the molecular basis of potency and bias between G protein and β-arrestin signaling, with the aim of informing peptide ligand design.
